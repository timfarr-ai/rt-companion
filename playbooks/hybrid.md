# Hybrid — Playbook

The **MEDIUM-equity** middle of the spectrum. Seller has an existing loan **AND** real equity, so neither
pure play fits: Sub-To undersells (they want their equity, not a $10K walk), and Seller Finance ignores
the cheap loan that's already in place. **Hybrid = assume the existing loan + carry the seller's equity as
a note.**

**Qualify:** there's a loan **and** meaningful equity (`seller_net = price − loan − 6%` comfortably **>
$15K**) **and** the existing rate is **assumable / low (≤ ~6%)**. If the rate is 7%+, walk — the seller can
just sell conventionally and the loan isn't worth taking over.

```
 1 FIND  →  2 VERIFY RATE+BAL+EQUITY  →  3 CALL  →  4 OFFER  →  5 EMAIL  →  6 SIGN  →  7 SUBMIT
```

---

### Step 1 — Find

Open the **[Call Sheet](/rt-companion/briefings/latest.html)** — Hybrid rides inside the *Mortgage Takeover*
section (ranked lowest-equity → lowest-rate) and is **tagged Hybrid** where there's real equity sitting on
top of an assumable loan. Or search [BBC Lightning Leads](https://www.buyboxcartel.com/vip/lightning-leads).

Best fit: **1–3 unit multi-family** and SFH/condo where the owner bought a few years ago at a low rate but
still has equity. (MFH is uncapped.)

### Step 2 — Verify rate + balance + equity before you dial

Three things, in this order:

1. **Rate** — confirm it's **≤ ~6%** in PropStream. If it's 7%+, this isn't a Hybrid; pivot or pass.
2. **Balance** — PropStream (or get the mortgage statement). You need it to size the carry-back.
3. **Equity is real** — pull SOLD comps (see **[Comps & Pricing](/rt-companion/playbooks/comps-and-pricing.html)**)
   to confirm the value supports the equity you'll be carrying back.

Walk away if it's a **HELOC or reverse mortgage**.

### Step 3 — Call

These are agent-listed (real-equity sellers list with realtors). **Never say "hybrid" or "subject-to" out
loud** — say *"take over the loan + carry your equity."* Confirm the rate and balance first:

> "Hey, I'm calling about [address]. I'm guessing your seller's got an existing loan they're paying down —
> are they sitting on a low rate from a few years back, by any chance?"

Then pitch the structure:

> "Here's what I can do. I'll put $[cash] in your seller's pocket at close. I take over the existing loan
> and keep paying it — so they're off the mortgage. The remaining equity, about $[carry-back], I carry
> back at $[monthly]/month for [N] years, with a balloon at year [N]. They get the full asking price —
> just spread out — and they skip the realtor cut and the all-at-once capital-gains hit."

**Objections — say exactly this:**

> **"Won't the bank call the loan?"** — "We put it in a land trust, so title moves to the trust, not a new
> owner — there's no trigger. Banks don't call performing loans, and the Garn–St. Germain Act protects
> transfers into a trust."

> **"Why not just take cash?"** — "Compare it: a cash sale nets them their equity minus your 6% — call it
> [equity − commission]. My structure gives them cash now, a monthly payment, *and* a balloon that adds up
> to the full asking. They come out ahead."

> **"What if you stop paying?"** — "If I miss two payments the property reverts to your seller and they keep
> every payment I've made plus any work I've done. I've got real money in it."

> **"This is too complicated."** — "It's three numbers: cash now, a monthly, and a balloon — like a rental
> they don't have to manage. I'll put it in writing so their attorney can review it."

**Verbal yes** = *"the structure makes sense, let me run it by my seller"* → text the proposal within 60s.

### Step 4 — Offer

Three parts. **NOT the Seller-Finance +15% / 40%-of-rent method.** The carry-back is on the **equity**, and
the monthly is **negotiated**:

```
Buy price ≈ at / near asking      (e.g. $250,000)
 ├─ Cash / down at close          (e.g. $30,000 — negotiable; a Sub-To-style $5-10K also works)
 ├─ Assume the existing loan       (e.g. $144,000 — you pay the bank its PITI; 4+ legal ways to take it over)
 └─ Carry back the EQUITY note:    carry-back = price − down − loan balance     (e.g. $76,000)
        monthly to seller = NEGOTIATED (e.g. $400/mo) — keep it ≤ your cash-flow headroom (rent×0.80 − existing PITI)
        balloon (5yr typical) payout = carry-back − (monthly × months)          (e.g. $52,000)
```

**Flexibility is the edge.** Seller wants more cash now? Bump the down and trim the balloon — same total.
Seasonal monthlies are fine on big deals (the canonical $3.8M example ran $4K/mo slow season, $15K/mo peak).

Run it + get the verdict:
**[Offer Oven — Hybrid](/rt-companion/playbooks/offer-oven-calculator.html?otype=Hybrid)** → enter the loan
balance + existing payment in the Sub-To block; it returns **PROCEED / COUNTER / KILL**.

### Step 5 — Email the offer

Send right after the call — the **Hybrid Multi-Family email, verbatim** (fill the [brackets]). It shows the
seller what they net at asking, then lays out the 3-part alternative with the full breakdown:

```
Good Evening [Ali],
Thank you for taking the time to speak with me today. On the phone I mentioned that your client owes
[$144,000] on their mortgage. At their asking price, after paying a 5% listing commission, they walk away
with [$46,000]. If anybody negotiates or asks for concessions, it could be [$30-40k] net to your client.

Here's an alternative offer:
- Buy Price: [$250,000]
- Down Payment: [$30,000]
- Term Length: 5 years
- Payout on 5 years: [$52,000]
- Monthly Payments: [$400]/month

Here's how the [$250,000] breaks down. [$144,000] of it is owed on the existing mortgage, which I'd take
over and keep paying — the loan doesn't have to be assumable, there are 4+ well-established legal ways to
take over a mortgage. [$30,000] is a down payment, and I make payments on the remaining [$76,000]. My
payments are [$400]/month for 60 months, and the payout would be [$52,000] on the 60th month, which I'd
deliver by selling or refinancing.
Regards, [You]
```

### Step 6 — Get it under contract

**Use BBC auto-contracts (primary).** Open the lead's **🏦 Create Offer in BBC** → fill the offer → it
generates the contract and **Save to Pipeline**. HMHW reviews & signs creative auto-contracts for you.

**The structure = a takeover + a carry-back note.** The takeover portion uses a **Trust Acquisition**: a
trust attorney puts the property into a revocable living trust; you take **90–95%** beneficial interest, the
seller keeps **5–10%**, the **mortgage stays in their name**, and you make the payments. The land trust
keeps the deal clear of the due-on-sale clause (Garn–St. Germain). The seller's **equity** is then
documented as a **carry-back note** with its monthly + balloon.

*Fallback only (BBC contracts down):* the editable "Mortgage Takeover OR Seller Finance"
