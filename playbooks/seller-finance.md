# Seller Finance — Playbook

Seller owns **free & clear — no mortgage at all**. They become the bank: you pay over asking, put a
little down, and make a monthly payment to **them** with a balloon at the end. No loan to assume, no
bank to qualify with.

**Qualify:** **free & clear** property, sitting on market (DOM 90+), where a normal loan/cash buyer
can't make it pencil — so seller finance is the *only* way it sells. This is the **HIGH-equity** end of
the spectrum (Hybrid = medium, Sub-To = low).

```
 1 FIND  →  2 VERIFY F&C  →  3 CALL  →  4 OFFER  →  5 EMAIL  →  6 SIGN  →  7 SUBMIT
```

---

### Step 1 — Find

Open the **[Call Sheet](/rt-companion/briefings/latest.html)** → *Seller Finance* section. Or search
[BBC Lightning Leads](https://www.buyboxcartel.com/vip/lightning-leads) for the `sellerFinance` tag.

Two profiles to look for:

- **Checkmate** — multi-family **5+ units**, or a **2–4plex that's NOT retail-desirable** (no owner-occupant
  would FHA-buy it); **$200K–$1.4M**; tenant-occupied; **the mortgage payment would exceed the rent.**
  Small MFH **>$1M, 4–12 units, free & clear** = the capital-gains-motivated sweet spot.
- **Stale-SFH** — single-family, **under ~$500K**, sitting **90+ days** (150+ ideal). Sub-$140K → use the
  interest variant (Step 4).

Add a **"vacant" filter** where you can — a seller who lives there often needs the lump sum to buy their
next place, so vacant + 150 days on market is the strongest seller-finance target.

### Step 2 — Verify it's free & clear before you dial

Confirm **no mortgage** on the property: **[Propwire](https://propwire.com/search) (free) → Free & Clear**,
or PropStream → Mortgage Information → Free & Clear → Include. Equity is *inferred* from the BBC tag until
you confirm it — so verify before the call.

Then pull the **real rent** (this deal lives or dies on rent, not value): Zillow Rent Zestimate vs the BBC
rent pill. **Haircut asking rent to the real average** — if the gap is >20%, the lower number wins. (Course:
*"They're asking 1,700. Doesn't mean they're getting 1,700. I bet it's on average maybe $1,400."*)

### Step 3 — Call

These are agent-listed. **Lead conversational — do NOT open with the DSCR pitch.** Get them talking,
then drop the structure when the door opens.

> "Hey, I'm calling about [address] — what can you tell me about this one? How long's it been sitting?"

Listen. When it's time to make the offer, give the **one-liner**:

> "I'd give your client [asking +15%], 10% down, and a monthly payment of about 40% of whatever it rents
> for — on a 6-year balloon. They'd net well over asking, spread out, and dodge the capital-gains hit of a
> lump-sum sale."

**The DSCR-wall walkthrough is your *explanation*, not your opener** — reach for it when the agent asks
"why would my seller do this?" Walk them through why nobody else can buy it:

> "If I buy it with a loan, my lender needs 20% down and my payment lands around [PITI] — but it only
> rents for [rent]. No lender post-2008 qualifies a loan that loses money every month. If I buy it cash,
> I'm trading [price] for [net rent]/mo — a ~4% return, I'd do better in an index fund. And no
> owner-occupant's going to live in a tenant's rental. So no matter what kind of buyer you bring, it
> can't sell. That's why I'm proposing seller financing — not because I want to, but because it's the
> only structure that actually closes."

**Objections — say exactly this:**

> **"My client wants cash, not payments."** — "Totally fair. But compare what they actually keep: a cash
> offer nets them maybe [cash − tax − commission] after capital gains and your fee. With payments they
> avoid the tax entirely — we don't pay tax in the US on money we haven't received yet — so they net
> *more*, just over time."

> **"There are other offers."** — "Are those buyers clearing a DSCR loan at 7.25%? Because the rent here
> doesn't support that payment. I'd bet they fall out of contract."

> **"0% interest is too low."** — "The premium *is* the interest. I'm paying [asking +15%] — that over-ask
> works out to an effective 8–9% return for your client, baked into the price instead of a rate."

> **"What if you stop paying?"** — "If I miss two payments, the property deeds straight back to your client
> via a deed of trust — they keep my down payment and every renovation. They reclaim it without ever
> having to foreclose."

**Verbal yes** = *"let me run it by my seller"* + the agent agrees to forward your offer + a follow-up is
booked. That's the win — you're asking for the chance to present, not a commitment.

### Step 4 — Offer

```
offer            = asking + 15%        (the premium replaces interest — it's 0%)
down             = 10% of offer        (5-10%; the agent's commission is paid out of this down)
monthly to seller = 40% of rent        ← THE payment (the number the seller mentally checks)
balloon          = 72 months (6 years)
balloon payout   = offer − down − (monthly × 72)   (you refi/sell to pay it out)
```

**Sub-$140K interest variant:** offer ~$5K over asking · **6.5% interest** · ~$15K down · **5-yr balloon**,
secured by a deed of trust (instead of the big 0% premium).

**Open softer than you settle** — never blurt your final terms:

| Lever | Open at | Settle / underwrite at |
|---|---|---|
| Down | **6.5–7%** | **10%** (12% on cheap SFH) |
| Price | **at asking** | asking **+10–20%** |
| Balloon | **15 yr** | **floor 7** (5 for MFH) |

**Never give the seller BOTH an inflated price AND an interest rate — pick one.** If they fixate on rate,
the third lever is to **stretch amortization to 35–40 years** so the payment shrinks while the down stays
low.

### Step 5 — Email the offer

Send right after the call — Richard's **Checkmate Pitch email, verbatim** (fill the [brackets]). It walks
the agent through the same DSCR-wall logic, then lands the offer:

```
Good Evening [Lisa],
Thank you for taking the time to speak with me today about your listing on [N Watts St]. I'm interested
in making you an offer as close to your asking price as I possibly can. I initially intended to offer
[$510,000], however if I get a mortgage on the property at that price, I'm paying [$3,300]/month while it
rents for [$3,600]. After management, vacancy and repairs, I'm losing money every month, and no lender
post-2008 will qualify a loan that doesn't cash-flow.

If I buy it in cash, I'm trading [$600,000] for about [$2,100]/month after expenses — roughly a 4% return.
I'd do better in the stock market. And no owner-occupant is going to live in a tenant-occupied rental.
So no matter what kind of buyer approaches you, this is very hard to sell conventionally.

As such, I'd like to present an offer on seller-financing terms — not because I prefer it, but because
it's the only structure I can see that actually closes. If you know another way, please tell me.

- Buy Price: [$700,000]  (my interest is built into the price — about [$100k] over 72 months)
- Down Payment: [$75,000] + closing costs ([$16,000] of this goes to you for commission)
- Monthly Payment: [$1,000]/month (the property nets ~[$2,100]; my down needs to buy real cash flow)
- Term & balloon: 72 months. If I miss 2 payments, the property is deeded back to your client and they
  keep my renovations and down payment. We secure this with a deed of trust.
- Balloon: on the 72nd month I refinance your client out their remaining [$553,000].

I know your client wasn't looking to seller finance — neither was I. If you have a better way to get this
done, I'm all ears. My proof of funds and portfolio are attached.
Regards, [You]
```

Cash-rich seller fixated on a lump sum? Pair it with the **cash + seller-finance** double-offer (Offer 1:
~70% cash via DSCR; Offer 2: the seller-finance terms above) so they self-select.

### Step 6 — Get it under contract

**Use BBC auto-contracts (primary).** Open the lead's **🏦 Create Offer in BBC** (the card button) → fill
the offer → it generates the creative contract and **Save to Pipeline**. HMHW reviews & signs creative
auto-contracts for you.

**Standard terms to set:** EMD **$1,000–1,500 (or 1% of price)**, submitted **after the contractor walk /
inspection** · **inspection 5 business days** · **COE 30 days** · down **5–10%, agent commission paid from
the down** · closing-attorney field = **"TBD at escrow"** · sign as **your own name / LLC — NEVER Grand In
Taylor**. The seller's protection is a **deed of trust**: miss two payments and the property reverts to them.

*Fallback only (BBC contracts down):* the editable "Mortgage Takeover OR Seller Finance"
