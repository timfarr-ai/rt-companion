Seller Finance — Playbook

Seller owns free & clear — no mortgage at all. They become the bank: you pay over asking, put a little down, and make a monthly payment to them with a balloon at the end. No loan to assume, no bank to qualify with.

Qualify: free & clear property, sitting on market (DOM 90+), where a normal loan/cash buyer can't make it pencil — so seller finance is the only way it sells. This is the HIGH-equity end of the spectrum (Hybrid = medium, Sub-To = low).

 1 FIND  →  2 VERIFY F&C  →  3 CALL  →  4 OFFER  →  5 EMAIL  →  6 SIGN  →  7 SUBMIT

Step 1 — Find

Open the Call Sheet → Seller Finance section. Or search BBC Lightning Leads for the sellerFinance tag.

Two profiles to look for:

  • Checkmate — multi-family 5+ units, or a 2–4plex that's NOT retail-desirable (no owner-occupant would FHA-buy it); $200K–$1.4M; tenant-occupied; the mortgage payment would exceed the rent. Small MFH >$1M, 4–12 units, free & clear = the capital-gains-motivated sweet spot.
  • Stale-SFH — single-family, under ~$500K, sitting 90+ days (150+ ideal). Sub-$140K → use the interest variant (Step 4).

Add a "vacant" filter where you can — a seller who lives there often needs the lump sum to buy their next place, so vacant + 150 days on market is the strongest seller-finance target.

Step 2 — Verify it's free & clear before you dial

Confirm no mortgage on the property: Propwire (free) → Free & Clear, or PropStream → Mortgage Information → Free & Clear → Include. Equity is inferred from the BBC tag until you confirm it — so verify before the call.

Then pull the real rent (this deal lives or dies on rent, not value): Zillow Rent Zestimate vs the BBC rent pill. Haircut asking rent to the real average — if the gap is >20%, the lower number wins. (Course: "They're asking 1,700. Doesn't mean they're getting 1,700. I bet it's on average maybe $1,400.")

Step 3 — Call

These are agent-listed. Lead conversational — do NOT open with the DSCR pitch. Get them talking, then drop the structure when the door opens.

"Hey, I'm calling about [address] — what can you tell me about this one? How long's it been sitting?"

Listen. When it's time to make the offer, give the one-liner:

"I'd give your client [asking +15%], 10% down, and a monthly payment of about 40% of whatever it rents for — on a 6-year balloon. They'd net well over asking, spread out, and dodge the capital-gains hit of a lump-sum sale."

The DSCR-wall walkthrough is your explanation, not your opener — reach for it when the agent asks "why would my seller do this?" Walk them through why nobody else can buy it:

"If I buy it with a loan, my lender needs 20% down and my payment lands around [PITI] — but it only rents for [rent]. No lender post-2008 qualifies a loan that loses money every month. If I buy it cash, I'm trading [price] for [net rent]/mo — a ~4% return, I'd do better in an index fund. And no owner-occupant's going to live in a tenant's rental. So no matter what kind of buyer you bring, it can't sell. That's why I'm proposing seller financing — not because I want to, but because it's the only structure that actually closes."

Objections — say exactly this:

"My client wants cash, not payments." — "Totally fair. But compare what they actually keep: a cash offer nets them maybe [cash − tax − commission] after capital gains and your fee. With payments they avoid the tax entirely — we don't pay tax in the US on money we haven't received yet — so they net more, just over time."

"There are other offers." — "Are those buyers clearing a DSCR loan at 7.25%? Because the rent here doesn't support that payment. I'd bet they fall out of contract."

"0% interest is too low." — "The premium is the interest. I'm paying [asking +15%] — that over-ask works out to an effective 8–9% return for your client, baked into the price instead of a rate."

"What if you stop paying?" — "If I miss two payments, the property deeds straight back to your client via a deed of trust — they keep my down payment and every renovation. They reclaim it without ever having to foreclose."

Verbal yes = "let me run it by my seller" + the agent agrees to forward your offer + a follow-up is booked. That's the win — you're asking for the chance to present, not a commitment.

Step 4 — Offer

offer            = asking + 15%        (the premium replaces interest — it's 0%)
down             = 10% of offer        (5-10%; the agent's commission is paid out of this down)
monthly to seller = 40% of rent        ← THE payment (the number the seller mentally checks)
balloon          = 72 months (6 years)
balloon payout   = offer − down − (monthly × 72)   (you refi/sell to pay it out)

Sub-$140K interest variant: offer ~$5K over asking · 6.5% interest · ~$15K down · 5-yr balloon, secured by a deed of trust (instead of the big 0% premium).

Open softer than you settle — never blurt your final terms:

Lever Open at Settle / underwrite at
Down 6.5–7% 10% (12% on cheap SFH)
Price at asking asking +10–20%
Balloon 15 yr floor 7 (5 for MFH)

Never give the seller BOTH an inflated price AND an interest rate — pick one. If they fixate on rate, the third lever is to stretch amortization to 35–40 years so the payment shrinks while the down stays low.

Run it + get the verdict: Offer Oven — Seller Finance → returns PROCEED / COUNTER / KILL and the buyer's cash-on-cash.

Step 5 — Email the offer

Send right after the call. This is the master Seller Finance offer email — lead with the offer, then the reasoning the agent uses to present it to the seller. The Offer Oven (deal type = Seller Finance) auto-fills every figure and generates this exact email, so you don't hand-calculate. Bracketed items are the fills; the [ … ] optional lines drop out when you have no competing-offer intel.

Source: Richard's seller-finance deck (official HMHW) + bedded down on the 13112 Canonbury St deal (2026-06-17). Token spec: playbooks/templates/seller-finance-offer.md.

Subject: LOI - [address]

Hi [agent],

Thanks for your time on the phone. Here's my offer in writing. I have flexibility in the terms
depending on your seller's needs[, and the means to take down multiple properties from your
seller's portfolio, but let's strike terms on this one first].

  - Price:      [offer = asking +10-15%]
  - Down:       [10% of offer]
  - Payment:    [≈40% of rent, the lion's share of NET]/month
  - Balloon:    ~[offer − down − (payment × term)] at month 72
  - EMD:        [1% of price, floor $1,000] non-refundable, submitted after inspection
  - Inspection: 7 business days
  - COE:        on or before 30 days
  - Security:   deed of trust / quit claim deed

My proof of funds is attached, I can close fast, and no appraisal is needed.

Here's the reasoning, so you can walk your seller through it:

I'd love to buy this the conventional way, and I'm sure your seller would prefer a clean sale.
But given how long it's been on the market, we both know that route isn't landing. There's a
structural reason: an investment property only makes sense if it cash flows, and at this rent it
won't support a loan much above [value the rent supports].

So financing fails above that[, which is why the [competing offer] fell through]. And cash won't
reach your number either: a cash buyer can earn 10%+ in the stock market, so they'll only buy
at a discount steep enough to beat it[, which is why offers are stuck around [cash level]].
Either way your client loses equity. It's a structural problem, with interest rates the highest
they've been in 25 years.

That's why I'm proposing seller financing. It's not my first choice either, but it's the only
structure that works here. You're paid your full commission out of my down payment, and your
client gets their full equity, above asking, while deferring the tax hit. The premium I pay over
list is their return, and the monthly payment is the lion's share of what the property nets after
taxes, insurance and upkeep. I cover every other closing cost too.

At the end of the term I refinance or sell them out of the balance. I set that term deliberately:
the property needs time to appreciate so I can refinance and pay your client in full. A shorter
balloon would risk leaving them short. And your client carries no risk. A deed of trust protects
them throughout: if I miss 60 days the property reverts to them, and they keep every payment
made, with no foreclosure.

They cease to be the landlord: no tenants, no repairs, no vacancy. Just paid like the bank, every
month, occupied or not.

Regards,
[you]

Cash-rich seller fixated on a lump sum? Pair it with the cash + seller-finance double-offer (Offer 1: ~70% cash via DSCR; Offer 2: the seller-finance terms above) so they self-select.

Step 6 — Get it under contract

Use BBC auto-contracts (primary). Open the lead's 🏦 Create Offer in BBC (the card button) → fill the offer → it generates the creative contract and Save to Pipeline. HMHW reviews & signs creative auto-contracts for you.

Standard terms to set: EMD $1,000–1,500 (or 1% of price), submitted after the contractor walk / inspection · inspection 5 business days · COE 30 days · down 5–10%, agent commission paid from the down · closing-attorney field = "TBD at escrow" · sign as your own name / LLC — NEVER Grand In Taylor. The seller's protection is a deed of trust: miss two payments and the property reverts to them.
