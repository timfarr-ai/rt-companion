🏛 Lender-Guideline References — the trust / DTI authority

Primary sources behind the MT/Trust-Acquisition "off your DTI" close. When a seller (or their loan officer) asks "won't this loan staying in my name block my next purchase?" — these are the actual rules Richard points to, not opinion. Full handling in the MT playbook → Objection 6.

🏦 Fannie Mae — B3-6-05, Monthly Debt Obligationsofficial
The "mortgage paid by another party" rule. If someone else makes the payment, the full housing payment can be excluded from the borrower's DTI — provided you document 12 months of canceled checks / bank statements with no late payments. This is the GSE rule that lets a subject-to/trust seller qualify for their next property as if the loan weren't there. ⚠ No trust/LLC carve-out shortens the 12 months — the trust solves due-on-sale, not the DTI timing.
Fannie Mae B3-6-05 ↗
🏦 Freddie Mac — Seller/Servicer Guide §5401.2official
Freddie's equivalent (Monthly debt payment-to-income ratio — debt paid by others). Same standard: 12 months of documented payments by the other party to exclude the debt from DTI. Cite both agencies together on calls — "Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac" — for maximum authority.
Freddie Mac §5401.2 ↗
⚖️ Garn–St Germain Act — 12 U.S.C. §1701j-3(d)(8)statute
Why the trust survives the due-on-sale clause. A lender may not call a loan due on "a transfer into an inter vivos trust in which the borrower is and remains a beneficiary and which does not relate to a transfer of rights of occupancy." This is exactly why the seller is kept on as a 5–10% beneficiary (buyer takes 90–95%). ⚠ The "rights of occupancy" clause is the soft spot — a real MT deal hands occupancy to the buyer, so protection is reduced, not bulletproof.
12 U.S.C. §1701j-3 ↗

The operational answer to the DTI objection. HMHW partnered with a nationwide loan originator, Andrew, who helps your seller get a new loan after they sell via the Trust Mortgage Takeover method — so "the rule allows it" becomes "and here's the lender who'll write your next loan."   1. Week 1: Watch all 3 YouTube courses + the Sebastian interview (~3.5 hrs total). Get the framework.
  2. Week 2: Listen to SF priority #1-4 (voicemail + 3 short pitches), then the 3 voicemail library clips. Loop the voicemails 5-10x in background.
  3. Before first call block: Listen to Pitch 26 (90-sec) + the SF voicemail one-liner. That's literally what you'll say.
  4. Ongoing: One full-length call per day (~25-40 min) during commute or workout. Rotate strategies.
