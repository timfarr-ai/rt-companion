Sub-To — Playbook

Seller has a mortgage and almost no equity. You take over their existing loan payment, hand them a small lump (often $0), and the loan stays in their name inside a trust. Fast, no new financing.

Use it when: there's a loan + the seller nets ≤ $15K at a sale (cornered). Call lowest-equity first — that's the most motivated seller.

STEP 2 — Verify before you dial

Confirm three things on the property: interest rate, loan balance, loan type. Skip it if the loan is a HELOC or reverse mortgage — those don't work.

🔧 PropStream (rate + liens + loan type) · Propwire (free, partial)

STEP 3 — The call

Open — ask about the loan (say this):

"If you've got a mortgage on it, I'd be happy to just take over your payments — a servicing company pays it for you so you never have to worry about it. If a payment is ever missed, the property comes right back to you and you keep everything paid up to that point. Since I'd run it as a rental, I just need the rate and balance to make sure it cash-flows — can you confirm those so my offer is solid?"

The Trojan Horse (if the agent won't entertain a takeover): make a cash offer designed to be rejected — loan balance + 4% of asking (nets the seller ~$0). When they say the seller can't make money at that price, you act surprised and pivot:

"There may actually be a creative way for your seller to walk away clean without losing money — would you be open to hearing it?"

→ then give the takeover/trust pitch.

Objections — say exactly this:

"Subject-to is illegal." → "It's standard — line 203 of the HUD-1 settlement statement is literally 'existing loan(s) taken subject to.' It's in Modern Real Estate Practice, Unit 12."

"The bank will call the loan (due-on-sale)." → "We put it in a land trust, so title moves to the trust, not a new owner — the bank has no trigger. They don't call performing loans."

"What if you stop paying?" → "If I miss two payments the property reverts to your seller and they keep every payment I've made. I've got real money in it — I'm not walking."

"Can my seller buy again after this?" → "Yes — I give them a trust certificate and an occupancy agreement showing they're covered. It resets their debt-to-income so they can qualify again."

Who buys it (set this now): if it cash-flows at the existing payment → an investor. If it doesn't (pricey + high rate) → $0 down and sell to someone who wants to live in it. Both are real deals.

STEP 5 — Get it signed

Trust Acquisition (this is the contract): the house goes into a revocable living trust via a trust attorney; the mortgage stays in the seller's name; you get 90–95% of the trust, the seller keeps 5–10%; you make the payments. Plus an Assignment Disclosure so you can assign it.

your text messages.
