# Sub-To — Playbook

Seller has a mortgage and **almost no equity**. You take over their existing loan payment, hand them a
small lump (often **$0**), and the loan stays in their name inside a trust. No new financing, fast close.

**Qualify:** there's a loan **and** the seller nets ≤ $15K at a sale (cornered). Call **lowest-equity
first** — most cornered = most motivated.

```
 1 FIND  →  2 VERIFY  →  3 CALL  →  4 OFFER  →  5 EMAIL  →  6 SIGN  →  7 SUBMIT
```

### Step 1 — Find

Open the **[Call Sheet](/rt-companion/briefings/latest.html)** → *Sub-To* section, already ranked
lowest-equity first. Or search [BBC Lightning Leads](https://www.buyboxcartel.com/vip/lightning-leads)
(on-market, equity ≤ 20%).

### Step 2 — Verify before you dial

Confirm on the property: **rate · balance · loan type**. Walk away if it's a **HELOC or reverse mortgage**.
Tool: PropStream (rate + liens) or [Propwire](https://propwire.com/search) (free).

### Step 3 — Call

Open by asking about the loan — **say this:**

> "If you've got a mortgage on it, I'd be happy to just take over your payments — a servicing company
> pays it for you so you never worry about it. If a payment's ever missed, the property comes right back
> to you and you keep everything paid up to that point. Since I'd run it as a rental, I just need the
> rate and balance to make sure it cash-flows — can you confirm those?"

If the agent won't entertain a takeover, run the **Trojan Horse**: make a cash offer built to be rejected
(`loan balance + 4% of asking` — nets the seller ~$0). When they say the seller can't make money at that
price, pivot:

> "There might actually be a creative way for your seller to walk away clean without losing money — would
> you be open to hearing it?"

**Objections — say exactly this:**

> **"Subject-to is illegal."** — "It's standard. Line 203 of the HUD-1 settlement statement is literally
> 'existing loan(s) taken subject to.' It's taught in Modern Real Estate Practice, Unit 12."

> **"The bank will call the loan."** — "We put it in a land trust, so title moves to the trust, not a new
> owner — there's no trigger. Banks don't call performing loans."

> **"What if you stop paying?"** — "If I miss two payments the property reverts to your seller and they
> keep every payment I've made. I've got real money in it — I'm not walking."

> **"Can my seller buy again after this?"** — "Yes — I give them a trust certificate and occupancy
> agreement. It resets their debt-to-income so they qualify again."

### Step 4 — Offer

| Existing rate | Cash to seller |
|---|---|
| 6%+ | **$0 down** |
| price under $150K | **$5K–12K lump** |
| pricier | **~5% down** |

Seller monthly ≈ **$0** (you pay the bank, not them). Run it + get the verdict:
**[Offer Oven — Sub-To](/rt-companion/playbooks/offer-oven-calculator.html?otype=Subto)** → returns
**PROCEED / COUNTER / KILL**.

**Who buys it:** cash-flows at the existing payment → an **investor**; if it doesn't (pricey + high rate)
→ **$0 down, sell to someone who wants to live in it.** Both are real deals.

### Step 5 — Email the offer

Send right after the call. **Copy-paste, fill the $$:**

```
Subject: [Address] — taking over the payments

Good evening [Name], thank you for your time today.

My lender won't budge much on [$130,000] — your seller would still lose $5–10k on a straight sale.
So instead I'll just take over their debt and let them qualify for a new loan down the road:

  • I take over whatever's owed and keep the mortgage current
  • [$8,500] to you, [$7,000] cash to your seller
  • I cover all closing costs

The loan isn't assumable, but here's the legal loophole: your client puts the property into a trust
created by an attorney and sells me the trust. It's a legal sale and resets their DTI completely.

Can we present this so your seller walks with [$7,000]? My portfolio and proof of funds are in your
text messages.

Regards,
[You]
```

### Step 6 — Sign

**Trust Acquisition** is the contract: the house goes into a revocable living trust (trust attorney); the
**mortgage stays in the seller's name**; you take **90–95%** of the trust, seller keeps **5–10%**; you make
the payments. Add an **Assignment Disclosure** so you can assign it.
[Contract — Mortgage Takeover / Seller Finance ↗](https://docs.google.com/document/d/19sgM1zTQSvDYbiCvEYjYsBUfb1bouPZA/edit)
